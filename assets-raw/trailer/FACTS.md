# Launch Window trailer: facts

Every fact a shot depicts, with its source (PLAN.md: history as it happened). An id here is what a
shot's `facts` names. **Checked** facts have a source; **to check** ones must be before their shot is
final.

## Checked

- **us-satellite-announcement-1955.** The White House announced on 29 July 1955 (after a closed briefing
  on the 28th) that the US would launch "small earth-circling satellites" for the International
  Geophysical Year. NASA, [International Geophysical Year](https://www.nasa.gov/history/sputnik/usannounce.html);
  [FRUS 1955–57 XI, 342](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1955-57v11/d342).
- **soviet-answer-1955.** News of the US plan reached the 6th International Astronautical Congress in
  Copenhagen (1–6 August 1955) on 2 August; Leonid Sedov held a press conference that day at the Soviet
  embassy in Copenhagen, about 50 journalists, and said a Soviet satellite could be expected in the near
  future. NASA, [Korolev, Sputnik, and the IGY](https://www.nasa.gov/history/sputnik/siddiqi.html);
  [Announcement of first artificial satellite](https://www.nasa.gov/history/monograph10/korspace.html).
- **embassy-reception-1957.** On 4 October 1957 a reception at the Soviet embassy in Washington closed
  the IGY's week-long rockets and satellites conference. Walter Sullivan (New York Times) got the news of
  Sputnik by phone shortly before 6 p.m. and passed it on; Lloyd Berkner quieted the room, announced it
  and congratulated the Soviet scientists; the guests raised their glasses. NASA,
  [Sputnik and the Origins of the Space Age](https://www.nasa.gov/history/sputnik/sputorig.html);
  [NASA, Vanguard history ch. 11](https://www.nasa.gov/history/sputnik/chap11.html).
- **vanguard-tv3.** On 6 December 1957 Vanguard TV3 rose a few feet at Cape Canaveral, lost thrust, fell
  back and exploded on its pad, on live television. Its satellite was thrown clear, landed in bushes near
  the pad and kept transmitting; it is in the National Air and Space Museum.
  [Astronomy.com](https://www.astronomy.com/today-in-the-history-of-astronomy/dec-6-1957-vanguard-tv3-explodes/);
  [Smithsonian, Satellite, Vanguard TV 3](https://si.edu/es/object/satellite-vanguard-tv-3:nasm_A19761857000).
- **flopnik-1957.** After Vanguard TV3's failure the press mocked it with Sputnik puns: "Flopnik", "Kaputnik",
  "Stayputnik", "Dudnik". NASA, [60 years ago, Vanguard fails to reach orbit](https://www.nasa.gov/feature/60-years-ago-vanguard-fails-to-reach-orbit);
  [Science News, Sputnik + 50](https://www.sciencenews.org/article/sputnik-50).
- **n1-1969.** On 3 July 1969 (Moscow time; already the 4th at Baikonur) the second N1 rose about
  200 metres from Site 110, fell back onto its pad and exploded, destroying the launch complex; 13 days
  before Apollo 11. US intelligence detected it; it was kept secret publicly.
  [The Space Review](https://thespacereview.com/article/3143/1);
  [Smithsonian Air & Space, Why the Soviets lost](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/air-space-magazine/apollo-why-the-soviets-lost-180972229/).

- **vostok-1.** Yuri Gagarin flew Vostok 1 on Wednesday, 12 April 1961: one full orbit (apogee about 327 km), 108
  minutes from launch to landing (106 also circulates). He wore an orange suit and a white helmet with "СССР" in red,
  painted on the morning of launch (who painted it is disputed: name no one). [NASM, Gagarin vs. Shepard](https://airandspace.si.edu/explore/stories/gagarin-vs-shepard);
  [RussianSpaceWeb, Vostok 1 launch](https://russianspaceweb.com/vostok1_launch.html).
- **freedom-7.** Alan Shepard flew Freedom 7 (Mercury-Redstone 3) on 5 May 1961 from Launch Complex 5 at Cape
  Canaveral, 23 days after Gagarin: suborbital, about 15 minutes. [NASA, Mercury-Redstone 3](https://nasa.gov/mission/mercury-redstone-3-freedom-7/);
  [NASA History, Shepard becomes first American in space](https://www.nasa.gov/history/60-years-ago-alan-shepard-becomes-the-first-american-in-space/).
- **friendship-7.** John Glenn flew Friendship 7 (Mercury-Atlas 6) on 20 February 1962: three orbits on an Atlas in
  4 h 55 min; over the Pacific at sunrise he saw the "fireflies" (ice particles). [NASA History, Glenn orbits Earth](https://www.nasa.gov/history/60-years-ago-john-glenn-the-first-american-to-orbit-the-earth-aboard-friendship-7/);
  [NASA Earth Observatory, What did John Glenn see?](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/what-did-john-glenn-see-77201/).
- **kennedy-rice-1962.** Kennedy spoke at Rice Stadium, Houston, on 12 September 1962, before about 40,000 people on a
  hot day (he joked about the heat). He had set the Moon goal before Congress on 25 May 1961; here he reaffirmed it,
  saying "We choose to go to the moon" twice in a row. NASA's recording is a US government work, public use permitted
  (use NASA's or the JFK Library's copy, not a broadcaster's). [NASA, Kennedy reaffirms Moon goal at Rice](https://www.nasa.gov/feature/60-years-ago-president-kennedy-reaffirms-moon-landing-goal-in-rice-university-speech);
  [American Presidency Project, Address at Rice University](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/address-rice-university-houston-the-nations-space-effort);
  [NTRS, NASA film of the speech](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19940010921).
- **vostok-6.** Valentina Tereshkova launched on Vostok 6 on 16 June 1963, the first woman in space; she flew nearly
  three days. [ESA, First woman in space](https://esa.int/About_Us/ESA_history/50_years_of_humans_in_space/First_woman_in_space_Valentina).
- **voskhod-2.** Alexei Leonov made the first spacewalk on 18 March 1965 from Voskhod 2, through an inflatable
  airlock on a 7 m tether, about 12 minutes outside. His suit stiffened and swelled, and he let air out of it to get
  back in (the suit's maker disputes "ballooned": say "stiffened"). White outer overalls; the helmet's markings are not
  confirmed by a source. [Zvezda, The First Spacewalk](https://artsandculture.google.com/exhibit/the-first-spacewalk/owJyBQMvbQy2JQ);
  [RussianSpaceWeb, Voskhod 2](https://www.russianspaceweb.com/voskhod2.html).
- **gemini-8.** Gemini VIII (Armstrong, Scott) made the first docking of two spacecraft in orbit on 16 March 1966, its
  nose into the docking collar of an uncrewed Agena; a stuck thruster then forced an emergency landing.
  [NASA, Gemini VIII, the first docking in space](https://www.nasa.gov/history/55-years-ago-gemini-viii-the-first-docking-in-space/);
  [NASM, Target Adapter, Agena](https://airandspace.si.edu/collection-objects/target-adapter-agena/nasm_A19690080000).
- **losses-1967.** Apollo 1 burned on 27 January 1967 in a preflight test on the pad at Launch Complex 34, killing
  Grissom, White and Chaffee; on 24 April 1967 Vladimir Komarov died when Soyuz 1's parachutes failed. "Both sides lost
  a crew" is fair (a crew can be one). Grissom and Chaffee are buried at Arlington National Cemetery, White at West
  Point; Komarov's ashes are in the Kremlin Wall (to confirm with a source). The lamp in shot 26 and the wreaths in 27 are symbolic.
  [NASA, Apollo 1](https://www.nasa.gov/mission/apollo-1/); [RussianSpaceWeb, Soyuz-1](https://www.russianspaceweb.com/soyuz1.html).
- **f1-tests.** The F-1 was test-fired at Edwards AFB from 1962 and at Marshall from 1965. Shot 25's night firing is
  our choice, not a claim (no source gives a time of day).
  [NASA, first full-thrust F-1 test, 26 May 1962](https://www.nasa.gov/image-article/this-week-nasa-history-1st-full-thrust-long-duration-f-1-engine-test-may-26-1962).
- **apollo-8.** Apollo 8's Saturn V rolled out to Pad 39A on 9 October 1968 on the crawler (six to eight hours;
  no source gives the time of day: shot 27c's dawn is our choice). It launched on 21 December 1968 at 7:51 am EST under
  thin cirrus; Borman, Lovell and Anders reached lunar orbit early on 24 December, the first humans to orbit the Moon.
  They flew in coveralls, bare-handed. Anders took Earthrise (AS08-14-2383) on 24 December with a Hasselblad and a
  250 mm lens; it is public domain (credit NASA), published turned so the lunar horizon runs along the bottom.
  [NASA, Apollo 8 rolls out](https://www.nasa.gov/history/50-years-ago-apollo-8-rolls-out-to-the-pad/);
  [NASA SP-4029, Apollo 8 summary](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/SP-4029/Apollo_08a_Summary.htm);
  [National Archives, Earthrise](https://www.docsteach.org/documents/document/earthrise-apollo-8);
  [NASA, Inside the Apollo 8 spacecraft](https://www.nasa.gov/image-article/inside-apollo-8-spacecraft-december-1968/).
- **n1-secret.** The uncrewed N1 5L launched on 3 July 1969 at 23:18 Moscow time from Site 110 at Baikonur (after
  midnight locally); an engine's oxygen pump exploded at liftoff, the rocket rose about 200 m, its engines shut down,
  and it fell back onto the pad and exploded, wrecking it. It carried a modified Soyuz-type craft for lunar orbit
  (don't mention a lander mockup). Thirteen days before Apollo 11 by Moscow date. The Soviet Union first acknowledged
  its crewed Moon program in August 1989 (Izvestia): "twenty years" is fair. The CIA saw the failure and photographed
  the wreck from orbit. [RussianSpaceWeb, N1 5L](https://russianspaceweb.com/n1_5l.html);
  [Centennial of Flight, Soviet lunar program](https://centennialofflight.net/essay/SPACEFLIGHT/soviet_lunar/SP21.htm);
  [The Space Review](https://thespacereview.com/article/3143/1).
- **apollo-11.** Launched 16 July 1969 at 9:32 am EDT from Pad 39A, watched by about a million people on the beaches
  and roads; landed 20 July in the Sea of Tranquility. During the descent CAPCOM Charlie Duke called "60 seconds" of
  fuel left; then "Contact light" and "Houston, Tranquility Base here. The Eagle has landed." Armstrong stood on the
  footpad and stepped down onto the surface at 10:56 pm EDT on 20 July (02:56 UTC on 21 July): "That's one small step
  for (a) man; one giant leap for mankind." The "60 seconds" clip is cut from NASA's own landing excerpt
  (62283main_landing.wav, via the Wayback Machine; public domain; edited, not continuous time).
  [NASA, Apollo 11 overview](https://www.nasa.gov/history/apollo-11-mission-overview/);
  [ALSJ, the landing](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/alsj/a11/a11.landing.html);
  [ALSJ, the first step](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/alsj/a11/a11.step.html).
