# Launch Window trailer: facts

Every fact a shot depicts, with its source (PLAN.md: history as it happened). An id here is what a
shot's `facts` names. **Checked** facts have a source; **to check** ones must be before their shot is
final.

## Checked

- **us-satellite-announcement-1955.** The White House announced on 29 July 1955 (after a closed briefing
  on the 28th) that the US would launch "small earth-circling satellites" for the International
  Geophysical Year. NASA, [International Geophysical Year](https://www.nasa.gov/history/sputnik/usannounce.html);
  [FRUS 1955–57 XI, 342](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1955-57v11/d342).
- **soviet-answer-1955.** News of the US plan reached the 6th International Astronautical Congress in
  Copenhagen (1–6 August 1955) on 2 August; Leonid Sedov held a press conference that day at the Soviet
  embassy in Copenhagen, about 50 journalists, and said a Soviet satellite could be expected in the near
  future. NASA, [Korolev, Sputnik, and the IGY](https://www.nasa.gov/history/sputnik/siddiqi.html);
  [Announcement of first artificial satellite](https://www.nasa.gov/history/monograph10/korspace.html).
- **embassy-reception-1957.** On 4 October 1957 a reception at the Soviet embassy in Washington closed
  the IGY's week-long rockets and satellites conference. Walter Sullivan (New York Times) got the news of
  Sputnik by phone shortly before 6 p.m. and passed it on; Lloyd Berkner quieted the room, announced it
  and congratulated the Soviet scientists; the guests raised their glasses. NASA,
  [Sputnik and the Origins of the Space Age](https://www.nasa.gov/history/sputnik/sputorig.html);
  [NASA, Vanguard history ch. 11](https://www.nasa.gov/history/sputnik/chap11.html).
- **vanguard-tv3.** On 6 December 1957 Vanguard TV3 rose a few feet at Cape Canaveral, lost thrust, fell
  back and exploded on its pad, on live television. Its satellite was thrown clear, landed in bushes near
  the pad and kept transmitting; it is in the National Air and Space Museum.
  [Astronomy.com](https://www.astronomy.com/today-in-the-history-of-astronomy/dec-6-1957-vanguard-tv3-explodes/);
  [Smithsonian, Satellite, Vanguard TV 3](https://si.edu/es/object/satellite-vanguard-tv-3:nasm_A19761857000).
- **n1-1969.** On 3 July 1969 (Moscow time; already the 4th at Baikonur) the second N1 rose about
  200 metres from Site 110, fell back onto its pad and exploded, destroying the launch complex; 13 days
  before Apollo 11. US intelligence detected it; it was kept secret publicly.
  [The Space Review](https://thespacereview.com/article/3143/1);
  [Smithsonian Air & Space, Why the Soviets lost](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/air-space-magazine/apollo-why-the-soviets-lost-180972229/).
