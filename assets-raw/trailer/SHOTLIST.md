# Launch Window trailer: shot list (draft 1, 2026-11-21)

The film as text, for review before anything is rendered. Once agreed it becomes `shots.json`
(PLAN.md §3), each fact in `FACTS.md`. About 95 seconds, 30 shots: a trailer cuts fast, and the
montage beats run at 1.5–2 s a shot. Shots marked *(optional)* can go if the film runs long or the
overnight run gets too heavy.

Conventions: **C** a caption (place and date, lower left, the edit), **S** an English subtitle for
Russian text on screen, **Sound** what the clip or the edit carries under the score. No real face is
shown (visors, backs, silhouettes, hands). Craft come from the user's mission configuration art as
references (PLAN.md §7).

The score carries the arc: a low pulse under the cold open, building through the satellite beat,
the first big swell at Gagarin, a drive through the middle years, silence for 1967, a swell for
Earthrise, a hard stop for the N1, and the long final build to the boot.

Total: about 95 s (91 s without the optional shots).

## Questions for the user

1. **Real NASA voices.** Besides "one small step": "60 seconds" and "The Eagle has landed" under 27–28? They add a lot and are public domain (to confirm), like the step.
2. **Kennedy.** "We choose to go to the Moon" (Rice University, 12 September 1962), also a real public-domain recording, could ride under 15–18. It's the most famous line of the race, but it is a voice-over in a film that has none so far. In or out?
3. **1967.** Shot 21 marks the losses with two empty pads and wreaths. Is that the right tone for a game trailer, or should the film skip the losses?
4. **Tereshkova (Vostok 6, June 1963), the first woman in space**, isn't in the list: one more Soviet First in the middle years if you want her.
5. **Length:** 30 shots at about 2 takes each makes 60 final renders, about 10 hours. The optional shots keep the overnight run in reach.
